4. Contrast: which colors work together?

Contrast measures how clearly foreground text stands out from its background. For WCAG AA, normal text needs at least 4.5:1; large text needs 3:1. Large text means approximately 24 CSS pixels regular or 18.7 CSS pixels bold—not simply text labelled as a “heading.” 

The ratios below are calculated from your exact hex values, using solid colors at full opacity.

Text color	Background	Contrast	Use
Deep ink #14342B	Warm ivory #FBF8F2	12.73:1	Excellent default for headings and body text.
Muted eucalyptus #52675D	Warm ivory #FBF8F2	5.73:1	Passes for secondary text.
Forest green #176849	Warm ivory #FBF8F2	6.36:1	Passes for headings, labels, and links.
Warm ivory #FBF8F2	Forest green #176849	6.36:1	Passes for primary button labels.
Bronze #98560B	Warm ivory #FBF8F2	5.40:1	Passes for gold-toned text.
Deep ink #14342B	Sage #E7F0E8	11.59:1	Excellent for reflection cards.
Light gold #F7D16C	Deep emerald #002B20	10.46:1	Excellent for short highlights on dark backgrounds.
Rich gold #D99A2E	Warm ivory #FBF8F2	2.30:1	Fails normal and large text contrast. Keep decorative.
Soft jade #569878	Warm ivory #FBF8F2	3.22:1	Passes large text only; avoid for body text.

Two important boundaries: your pale border color is decorative, not a sufficient standalone selection indicator. Use a forest-green outline plus a checkmark or label for selected companions. Essential visual indicators need adequate contrast, and meaning should not depend on color alone. 

For reel subtitles, place text on a solid or sufficiently opaque panel; the contrast of these palette pairs does not guarantee readability over changing footage.
